# Message Strategies Library (MS1-MS50)

Curated for 7 and 8-figure Shopify e-commerce brands.

This file is the shared persuasion vocabulary for all creative work.
Every persona profile, every script, every hook, and every objection
crusher should cite one or more MS numbers from this library.

Read it before producing or evaluating script copy, hooks, or offer
positioning.

---

## How to Cite an MS

- In a persona profile: `Best MS for this persona: MS7, MS14, MS28`
- In a script brief: `Framework: Hook-Story-Offer. MS in play: MS3
  (Mechanism Reveal) + MS28 (Concrete) + MS32 (Specificity Over
  Claims)`
- In a QC comment: `Avatar was assigned MS18 (Superiority/Status)
  but script only lands MS1. Rewrite body to include the premium
  positioning angle.`

---

## The Master Formula

Every ad is built from this combination: **WHO + WHERE + HOW = AD**

- **WHO** = the customer persona
- **WHERE** = their awareness stage and sophistication level
- **HOW** = a message strategy (this file) + an angle + a script
  structure + a format

Pick one of each. That is one ad. Vary any single input to produce a
new ad.

---

## How to Pick the Right MS

1. Identify the persona's awareness stage. Filter MS strategies by
   awareness stage.
2. Identify the market sophistication level. Filter further by
   Sophistication Level.
3. Identify the angle you want to lead with. Filter further by Best
   Angles.
4. From the remaining 3-8 strategies, pick the one whose Universal
   Framework gives you the strongest hook.

If the strategy doesn't naturally produce a sharp first-line hook,
it is the wrong strategy for that ad.

# SCHWARTZ — Copy Escalation (MS1-MS6)

As markets mature, escalate your copy from simple claim to mechanism
to proof to identity to story. The level of your copy must match the
sophistication of your market.

### MS1: Direct Claim
State the core benefit simply and directly. No proof needed. No
mechanism. Just the promise. Use only when the market has never
heard the claim before.
- Awareness: Unaware, Problem Aware | Sophistication: 1-2
- Angles: Problem, Education
- Framework: "[Product] does [core benefit]."

### MS2: Amplified Claim
Take the same claim and add specifics: numbers, scope, timeframe,
material grade. Make the promise bigger and more concrete than the
leader is making it.
- Awareness: Problem Aware, Solution Aware | Sophistication: 2-3
- Angles: Problem, Mechanism
- Framework: "[Product] does [core benefit]. [Specific
  number/detail] better/faster/stronger."

### MS3: Mechanism Reveal
Do not just claim the benefit. Explain HOW it works. The mechanism
is the reason behind the claim. This is what separates you from
everyone making the same promise.
- Awareness: Solution Aware | Sophistication: 3-4
- Angles: Mechanism, Comparison
- Framework: "Here is WHY [product] works when others do not:
  [mechanism explanation]."

### MS4: Proof-Stacked Mechanism
Mechanism alone is not enough for jaded customers. Stack proof on
top of the mechanism: patent, certification, reviews, press, demo.
The mechanism explains. The proof proves.
- Awareness: Solution Aware, Product Aware | Sophistication: 4
- Angles: Mechanism, Social Proof
- Framework: "[Mechanism] + [Proof 1] + [Proof 2] + [Proof 3]."

### MS5: Identity Wrap
The product becomes a statement about who the customer IS. Not what
it does, what it says about them. This is for markets so
sophisticated that claims and proof feel like noise.
- Awareness: Product Aware, Most Aware | Sophistication: 4-5
- Angles: Identity, Story
- Framework: "For the [identity] who [defining behavior or belief]."

### MS6: Story Transcendence
The ad does not feel like an ad. Pure narrative: founder journey,
customer transformation, or cultural moment. The product appears
inside the story, not as the point of it.
- Awareness: Any | Sophistication: 5
- Angles: Story, Identity
- Framework: "[Narrative that contains the product without selling
  it directly.]"

# CIALDINI — Influence Triggers (MS7-MS13)

Universal principles of persuasion that work because they are wired
into human psychology. Each trigger can be deployed independently or
stacked.

### MS7: Social Proof - Numbers
Lead with volume: review count, user count, view count, sales count.
Quantity signals safety. If that many people chose it, it must be
good.
- Awareness: Solution Aware, Product Aware | Sophistication: 2-4
- Angles: Social Proof
- Framework: "[Large number] [people] [did thing]. Here is why."

### MS8: Social Proof - Similarity
People follow people LIKE THEM. Show a customer who shares the
target's identity: same frustration, same situation, same values.
More powerful than a celebrity endorsement.
- Awareness: Solution Aware | Sophistication: 3-4
- Angles: Social Proof, Identity
- Framework: "[Person just like you] tried [product] and [result]."

### MS9: Authority Transfer
Borrow credibility from institutions, publications, experts, or
retailers. The audience may not trust YOU yet, but they trust the
institution.
- Awareness: Solution Aware, Product Aware | Sophistication: 3-4
- Angles: Social Proof, Mechanism
- Framework: "[Authority] [endorsed/featured/stocks] this. [What
  that signals.]"

### MS10: Scarcity / Urgency
Limited availability, first drop, seasonal, or genuine constraint.
Works because loss aversion is stronger than desire for gain. MUST
be real. Fake scarcity destroys trust.
- Awareness: Most Aware, Product Aware | Sophistication: 1-3
- Angles: Urgency
- Framework: "[Limited thing] is [available/ending] [when].
  [Action]."

### MS11: Commitment Ladder
People who take one small step are far more likely to take the next.
If they bought one product, they are primed for the next. Leverage
existing commitment.
- Awareness: Product Aware, Most Aware | Sophistication: 2-3
- Angles: Identity, Social Proof
- Framework: "You already [small commitment]. [Next step] is
  [natural extension]."

### MS12: Reciprocity
Give genuine value first: useful information, a guide, tips, honest
comparison. THEN make the ask. People feel compelled to return
favors.
- Awareness: Unaware, Problem Aware | Sophistication: 3-5
- Angles: Education, Story
- Framework: "Here is [genuinely useful thing] for free. [Soft
  mention of product.]"

### MS13: Liking / Founder Connection
We buy from people we like. Founder stories, relatability, shared
values, humor: all create liking. The purchase becomes personal, not
transactional.
- Awareness: Any | Sophistication: 4-5
- Angles: Story, Identity
- Framework: "[Real human story that makes the brand likeable and
  relatable.]"

# WHITMAN — Core Desires (MS14-MS19)

Primal desires hardwired into every human. These are not wants, they
are NEEDS. Tap into the right one and the customer feels the pull
before they think about it.

### MS14: Protect Loved Ones
The most powerful desire for parents. Trigger the instinct to keep
their child safe. Material safety, chemical-free, tested. Also
applies to caregivers, pet owners, anyone responsible for someone
vulnerable.
- Awareness: Problem Aware, Solution Aware | Sophistication: 2-4
- Angles: Problem, Mechanism
- Framework: "Your [loved one] [uses/touches/eats from] this
  [frequency]. What is it made from?"

### MS15: Freedom from Fear
Remove anxiety. Name the fear, then dissolve it. Customers carry
fear about chemicals, safety, milestones, judgment, failure. Offer
relief.
- Awareness: Problem Aware | Sophistication: 2-3
- Angles: Problem, Education
- Framework: "Stop worrying about [fear]. [How product removes it.]"

### MS16: Social Approval
People want to be admired, noticed, respected. Shows up as having
gear that looks good, choices that feel elevated, decisions other
people in the reference group will respect.
- Awareness: Solution Aware, Product Aware | Sophistication: 3-4
- Angles: Identity, Social Proof
- Framework: "The [product] that [reference group] notices."

### MS17: Comfortable Living
Paint the peaceful 'after.' No mess. No stress. The daily task as a
calm, enjoyable part of the day instead of a battle.
- Awareness: Problem Aware, Solution Aware | Sophistication: 1-3
- Angles: Problem, Identity
- Framework: "Imagine [stressful situation] but [peaceful version]."

### MS18: Superiority / Status
Premium positioning. The customer is choosing quality because they
can. Not the budget option. The considered, elevated choice.
- Awareness: Solution Aware, Product Aware | Sophistication: 3-5
- Angles: Identity, Comparison
- Framework: "Not [cheap alternative]. [Premium alternative that
  signals taste/standards]."

### MS19: Nurture
Caring for little ones. Feeding them well. Watching them grow. Tap
into the tenderness that makes people spend without blinking. Heavy
in baby, pet, plant, and food categories.
- Awareness: Problem Aware, Product Aware | Sophistication: 1-3
- Angles: Story, Education
- Framework: "Designed for [their little hands/growing
  body/developmental stage]."

# MILLER — StoryBrand (MS20-MS25)

The customer is the HERO. The brand is the GUIDE. Every story has an
external problem (visible), internal problem (emotional), and
philosophical problem (moral). Name all three.

### MS20: Name the External Problem
The visible, tangible, surface-level problem. What can you SEE going
wrong? This is the entry point.
- Awareness: Problem Aware | Sophistication: 1-3
- Angles: Problem
- Framework: "[Visible problem]. [What it looks like in daily
  life.]"

### MS21: Name the Internal Problem
The emotional problem BEHIND the visible one. The frustration,
guilt, embarrassment, self-doubt. This is what actually drives the
purchase.
- Awareness: Problem Aware, Solution Aware | Sophistication: 3-4
- Angles: Problem, Identity
- Framework: "The real problem is not [external]. It is [internal
  feeling]."

### MS22: Name the Philosophical Problem
The 'ought to.' The moral principle underneath. It is not just
frustrating, it is WRONG. This is the level that turns customers
into advocates.
- Awareness: Solution Aware | Sophistication: 4-5
- Angles: Identity, Education
- Framework: "It should not be this way. [What OUGHT to be true.]"

### MS23: Position as Guide
You are not the hero, they are. You are the guide: someone who has
EMPATHY (been there) and AUTHORITY (solved it). Show both.
- Awareness: Solution Aware, Product Aware | Sophistication: 3-5
- Angles: Story, Social Proof
- Framework: "We had [same problem]. We [solved it]. Here is how."

### MS24: Give a Plan
Reduce complexity to 3-4 steps. The easier it looks, the more likely
they act. The plan removes 'what do I do next?' anxiety.
- Awareness: Product Aware, Most Aware | Sophistication: 2-3
- Angles: Education, Urgency
- Framework: "Step 1: [Easy action]. Step 2: [Simple next step].
  Step 3: [Result]."

### MS25: Paint Success and Failure
Show both futures. The 'after' with your product AND the 'staying
same' without it. Contrast creates urgency.
- Awareness: Problem Aware, Solution Aware | Sophistication: 2-4
- Angles: Problem, Comparison
- Framework: "WITH [product]: [success picture]. WITHOUT: [failure
  picture]."

# HEATH & HEATH — Made to Stick (MS26-MS31)

Why do some ideas stick and others die? Simple, Unexpected,
Concrete, Credible, Emotional, Stories. Each makes your message
stickier.

### MS26: Simple
Strip the message to its absolute core. One benefit. No jargon. No
feature list. If you can only say ONE thing about the product, what
is it?
- Awareness: Any | Sophistication: 1-2
- Angles: Problem
- Framework: "[One sentence. One benefit. Done.]"

### MS27: Unexpected
Break a pattern the audience holds. Say something they do not
expect. Violate their mental schema. The surprise creates attention;
the insight creates retention.
- Awareness: Solution Aware | Sophistication: 3-5
- Angles: Comparison, Education
- Framework: "[Contrarian statement that violates expectations.]"

### MS28: Concrete
Make it tangible. Numbers, specifics, sensory details. Abstract
claims die. Concrete details stick.
- Awareness: Solution Aware, Product Aware | Sophistication: 2-4
- Angles: Mechanism, Comparison
- Framework: "[Specific number] [specific unit]. That is [what it
  means in real terms]."

### MS29: Credible
Stack evidence until doubt is unreasonable. At high sophistication,
one proof point is not enough. You need a pile of different TYPES of
proof.
- Awareness: Product Aware | Sophistication: 3-5
- Angles: Social Proof, Mechanism
- Framework: "[Proof 1] + [Proof 2] + [Proof 3] + [Proof 4]. [That
  is the evidence.]"

### MS30: Emotional
Make them FEEL it before they think about it. Not the product's
features, the customer's lived experience. The exhaustion. The
relief.
- Awareness: Problem Aware | Sophistication: 2-4
- Angles: Problem, Story
- Framework: "[Sensory, emotional description of their reality.]"

### MS31: Story
Wrap the message in a narrative. Stories are how humans process
information. Beginning, middle, end. Character, conflict,
resolution.
- Awareness: Any | Sophistication: 4-5
- Angles: Story, Identity
- Framework: "[Character] had [problem]. Then [turning point]. Now
  [resolution]."

# HOPKINS — Scientific Advertising (MS32-MS37)

Advertising is salesmanship in print. Every claim must be specific.
Every promise must be proved. Test everything. Assume nothing. Let
the product demonstrate itself.

### MS32: Specificity Over Claims
Replace adjectives with facts. 'Strong suction' means nothing.
Specific measurable detail means everything. Specific beats vague,
always.
- Awareness: Solution Aware | Sophistication: 2-4
- Angles: Mechanism
- Framework: "Not '[vague adjective].' [Specific measurable
  detail]."

### MS33: Reason-Why Copy
Do not just state what it does. Explain WHY it does it. The 'reason
why' is the mechanism in simple language.
- Awareness: Solution Aware | Sophistication: 3-4
- Angles: Mechanism, Education
- Framework: "[Claim]. Here is why: [reason-why explanation]."

### MS34: Demo / Test
Seeing beats telling. Video demo beats feature list, always. The
audience does not have to trust you if they can see it work.
- Awareness: Solution Aware, Product Aware | Sophistication: 3-5
- Angles: Mechanism, Social Proof
- Framework: "Watch [product] [doing the thing you claim]. [No
  narration needed.]"

### MS35: Risk Reversal
Remove the risk of trying. Guarantee, free return, money-back
promise. At high sophistication, the customer's biggest fear is not
the product, it is wasting money AGAIN.
- Awareness: Product Aware | Sophistication: 3-4
- Angles: Social Proof, Comparison
- Framework: "If [product] does not [promised result], [risk
  removal]."

### MS36: Track Record
Past performance as proof of future results. The brand's history,
existing product reviews, longevity, repeat purchases. Especially
powerful for new product launches from established brands.
- Awareness: Product Aware | Sophistication: 2-4
- Angles: Social Proof
- Framework: "[Brand] has [track record]. [New product] is
  [extension of that quality]."

### MS37: Head-to-Head Comparison
Compare directly to the alternative: by price, material, reviews,
features, longevity. Do not trash the competitor; lay facts side by
side and let the audience conclude.
- Awareness: Solution Aware | Sophistication: 3-4
- Angles: Comparison, Mechanism
- Framework: "[Competitor] vs [your product]: [fact] vs [fact],
  [fact] vs [fact]."

# HALBERT — Starving Crowd (MS38-MS43)

The #1 advantage in marketing is a 'starving crowd.' Find the people
in the most pain, with the most desire, and the fewest options. Then
feed them.

### MS38: Identify the Starving Crowd
Find the person in the MOST pain. Not the casual browser, the one
who has tried everything and given up. They are starving for a
solution that works.
- Awareness: Solution Aware | Sophistication: 4-5
- Angles: Problem, Comparison
- Framework: "If you have [painful history], this is for you."

### MS39: Unique Mechanism
The ONE thing that makes your solution different from everything
they have tried. Not a list of features, the single differentiator.
- Awareness: Solution Aware | Sophistication: 3-4
- Angles: Mechanism
- Framework: "The only [product/brand] that [unique differentiator].
  Because [reason]."

### MS40: Irresistible Offer Stack
Stack value beyond the product itself. Product + ecosystem +
certification + guarantee + story = an offer that feels like more
than a product.
- Awareness: Product Aware | Sophistication: 3-4
- Angles: Social Proof, Urgency
- Framework: "You get [product] + [bonus value 1] + [bonus value 2]
  + [guarantee]."

### MS41: Urgency From Truth
Real urgency, not manufactured scarcity. First production run. New
product launch. Genuine constraint creates genuine urgency.
- Awareness: Most Aware | Sophistication: 2-3
- Angles: Urgency
- Framework: "[Honest constraint]. [Why acting now matters.]"

### MS42: Future Pace
Make them LIVE in the future where they already own the product. The
transformation is real, tangible, emotional. They feel ownership
before they buy.
- Awareness: Problem Aware, Solution Aware | Sophistication: 2-3
- Angles: Problem, Identity
- Framework: "[Timeframe] from now, [what life looks like with the
  product]."

### MS43: Common Enemy
Unite the customer against a shared villain. Not a competitor brand,
a THING. Cheap materials. False marketing. The race to the bottom.
- Awareness: Solution Aware | Sophistication: 3-5
- Angles: Comparison, Story
- Framework: "[The villain] is the real problem. Not [what they
  think]."

# OGILVY — Brand and Long Copy (MS44-MS50)

Every ad should contribute to the long-term personality of the
brand. Brands with a defined character win. Ads with substance
outperform ads with tricks.

### MS44: Brand Personality
Inject character into the brand. Not a faceless company, a
personality with voice, values, and flaws. People buy from brands
they would want as friends.
- Awareness: Product Aware, Most Aware | Sophistication: 4-5
- Angles: Story, Identity
- Framework: "[Brand] is not [corporate thing]. It is [human
  thing]."

### MS45: Long-Form Education
Teach something genuinely useful. The content is the value, the
product is secondary. People who learn from you trust you.
- Awareness: Unaware, Problem Aware | Sophistication: 3-5
- Angles: Education
- Framework: "Everything you need to know about [topic]. (And yes,
  we sell [product], but this is useful regardless.)"

### MS46: Visual Proof
The image IS the ad. Show the product doing the thing you claim. A
photo says more than 500 words.
- Awareness: Solution Aware, Product Aware | Sophistication: 2-4
- Angles: Mechanism, Social Proof
- Framework: "[Image/video of product doing the thing]. [Minimal
  copy.]"

### MS47: The Confession
Admit a weakness to build trust. Radical honesty disarms skepticism.
In a market full of overclaiming, the brand that admits limits
becomes most trusted.
- Awareness: Solution Aware | Sophistication: 4-5
- Angles: Education, Comparison
- Framework: "Honestly? [Weakness]. But [compensating strength]."

### MS48: The Question Headline
Lead with a question that implies its own answer. The reader's brain
cannot help but engage. Questions that touch on identity, fear, or
curiosity work best.
- Awareness: Problem Aware, Solution Aware | Sophistication: 1-3
- Angles: Problem, Education
- Framework: "[Question that implies the answer you want them to
  reach.]"

### MS49: News Angle
Frame the product as news: a launch, a first, a breakthrough. People
pay attention to new things. Newsworthiness creates its own urgency.
- Awareness: Unaware, Product Aware | Sophistication: 1-3
- Angles: Urgency, Social Proof
- Framework: "Just [launched/discovered/announced]: [product news]."

### MS50: The Reversal
Flip the expected narrative. Start with the opposite of what they
expect to hear. The contrast creates attention and reframes the
product.
- Awareness: Problem Aware, Solution Aware | Sophistication: 3-5
- Angles: Comparison, Story
- Framework: "You do not need [product]. Unless [reveal that they
  actually do]."

# Selection Cheat Sheet by Sophistication Level

**Level 1 (virgin market):** MS1 (Direct Claim) or MS26 (Simple).
The first to make the claim wins.

**Level 2 (claim is being made):** MS2 (Amplified Claim), MS28
(Concrete), MS25 (Paint Success and Failure). Bigger, more specific,
more visceral than the leader.

**Level 3 (mechanism war):** MS3 (Mechanism Reveal), MS33
(Reason-Why Copy), MS39 (Unique Mechanism), MS37 (Head-to-Head).
Explain WHY yours works.

**Level 4 (mechanisms are saturated):** MS4 (Proof-Stacked
Mechanism), MS27 (Unexpected), MS47 (The Confession), MS43 (Common
Enemy). Innovate the mechanism or pivot the angle.

**Level 5 (everything has been said):** MS5 (Identity Wrap), MS6
(Story Transcendence), MS31 (Story), MS44 (Brand Personality), MS13
(Liking/Founder). Identity beats benefits.
