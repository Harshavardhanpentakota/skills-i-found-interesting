# Ad Creative Principles

Read this file before producing any ad creative or research.

---

## THE PRIME DIRECTIVE

Before producing any creative output, ask one question:

> **Would a cold customer — someone who knows nothing about this
> brand, has never heard of this product, and is mid-scroll on Meta
> — click to the next step?**

If the answer is no, or even "maybe," the output is not done.

This is not a creative opinion. This is the job. Every script, hook,
brief, and static concept must pass this test before it ships. Cold audience click behavior is the only metric that matters
at the top of the funnel.

---

## AD CREATIVE PHILOSOPHY

The goal is not pretty ads. It is assets that scale the business.
Every creative asset exists to drive performance, not to look good
in a portfolio.

Core approach:

- If your ad doesn't stop the scroll, nothing else matters.
- Performance over "premium." A solid ad that launches now beats a
  polished ad that launches late.
- Create from the customer's perspective. Solve their problems,
  speak their language.
- Clear messaging hierarchy. One message. One hook. One CTA.
- Scroll-stopping visual hooks in the first 2 seconds, no
  exceptions.
- Fast-paced, high-clarity edits. TikTok speed, not YouTube speed.
- Emotion or value conveyed in the first 3 seconds.
- Ads must feel native to the feed. If it looks like an ad,
  performance drops.

Creative is the #1 performance lever in paid media. Better creative
lowers CPA, improves CTR, extends ad lifespan, and scales spend.
Media buying cannot save weak creative. Targeting puts the ad in
front of people. Creative decides whether they care.

**Why most ads fail:**

- Weak hooks
- Poor pacing
- Confusing messages
- No clear CTA

Not because of budget, algorithm changes, or platform issues.

---

## UGLY ADS > POLISHED ADS

"Ugly ads" do not mean low-quality. They mean natural, relatable
content that looks like it was not professionally produced for
marketing.

- Authenticity over polish. Avoid obvious branding in the first
  frame. Users skip when they instantly detect an ad.
- Hook with relevance, not flash. Real-world scenarios and raw
  moments outperform glossy visuals.
- Platform-native execution. Use platform fonts, handheld filming,
  imperfect shots. Blend in with organic content.
- Brand fonts kill authenticity in UGC. Use native platform fonts
  (TikTok: Proxima Nova Semibold; Instagram: San Francisco).
- Ads with stickers, overlays, and native UI elements often
  outperform clean versions.
- 70% of Reels are now watched with sound on. Design for sound ON,
  not just captions.

Structure still matters. Ugly does not mean disorganized. Every ad
still needs a clear hook, story arc, and call to action.

**Speed mindset:** "Good enough" means strategy is sound, hook is
strong, specs are correct, and quality standards are met. It does
not mean careless. A beautiful ad that launches late is worse than a
solid ad that launches now.

## HOOK FRAMEWORK — NON-NEGOTIABLE

Hooks are the single most important element of any ad.

Do not guess hooks and do not rely on creative intuition alone.
Every hook originates from market research, not imagination.

**If the hook fails:**

- The ad does not get watched
- The creative does not scale
- Everything after it is irrelevant

### What a Hook Is

A hook is the first message the audience experiences that:

1. Calls out the right person
2. Signals value immediately
3. Creates curiosity to keep watching

A hook is NOT a clever slogan, branding statement, or vague
motivational copy. Hooks are strategic.

### Step 1: Start With Market Research (Non-Negotiable)

Every hook must originate from one of these three research anchors:

**Desire** — what the audience WANTS
> "I want my dog to run around like a puppy again"

**Symptom** — what the audience NOTICES happening
> "My dog is struggling to jump on the couch"

**Problem** — what the audience KNOWS is wrong
> "My dog has arthritis"

Rotate between all three angles. Do not build hooks on one angle
only.

### Step 2: Apply the Hook Formula

Every winning hook must include all three:

**1. Ideal Audience Callout**
Signal clearly who this is for. If it appeals to everyone, it
appeals to no one.
> "If your dog is struggling to jump..."

**2. Implied Benefit**
The viewer must feel: "If I keep watching, I'm going to get
something valuable."
> Bad: "You need to watch this"
> Good: "You need to see this 90-second fix"

**3. Intrigued Curiosity**
Create curiosity without being vague. The viewer should think: "I've
tried other things... this sounds different."
> "New Facebook ad strategy..." / "Because of this one thing..." /
> "This simple fix..."

### Hook Rules

- One hook per ad. One idea. One message. One angle.
- Hook must be clear in 1-3 seconds. Who it's for, what it's about,
  why they should care.
- The hook determines the rest of the ad. Weak hooks cannot be saved
  later.
- Video hooks: earn attention in 0-2 seconds. No slow intros, no
  logos first, no "Hi, my name is."
- Static hooks: understood in under 1 second. Hook must be visually
  dominant: large, high-contrast, impossible to ignore.

### Hook QA (Mandatory Before Any Output)

- Is the audience clearly called out?
- Is there an implied benefit?
- Is curiosity created without confusion?
- Does this come from real market research?
- Would the wrong audience ignore this?

If any answer is NO, revise before proceeding.

### Common Hook Failures (Unacceptable)

- No audience callout
- No implied benefit
- Curiosity without clarity
- Generic claims ("Scale your business," "Level up your brand")
- Hooks based on assumptions instead of research

These are strategy failures, not stylistic issues.

## MARKET SOPHISTICATION — 5 LEVELS (SCHWARTZ)

Awareness stage answers "what does my prospect know?" Market
sophistication answers "what has my prospect *heard before*?" Both
must be assigned to every persona and both determine how a hook,
claim, and mechanism must be pitched.

Sophistication compounds over time. A category that was Level 1 ten
years ago is Level 4 or 5 today. Running Level 2 copy in a Level 5
market produces a beautiful ad that does not convert.

**Level 1 — No one is making the claim yet.**
The market is virgin. Make the claim plainly. Biggest, boldest,
simplest statement wins.
- *Copy approach:* Plain benefit claim. No mechanism needed. No
  proof needed beyond the claim itself.

**Level 2 — Competitors are making the claim. Now you have to make
it bigger.**
Amplify the claim. Bigger promise, faster result, more specific
number.
- *Copy approach:* Superlative benefit. Competitive framing. Bigger
  promise than the leader.

**Level 3 — The market has heard the bigger claim. Now you need a
mechanism.**
Explain WHY your version of the claim is true. The mechanism becomes
the differentiator.
- *Copy approach:* Mechanism-led hooks. Reason-why copy.
  Specificity as credibility.

**Level 4 — The mechanism is now also saturated. You need a better
mechanism, or a new angle on the old one.**
Re-invent the mechanism, or pivot to a new angle entirely (identity,
experience, community).
- *Copy approach:* Mechanism innovation, identity-led hooks,
  pattern-interrupt contrarian claims.

**Level 5 — Everything has been said. The only thing that works is
identifying with the prospect at a deeper level.**
Pure identity, community, or worldview play. The product becomes
secondary to belonging.
- *Example:* "For women who lifted heavy in their 30s and aren't
  starting over in their 50s."
- *Copy approach:* Identity-first hooks. Belonging over benefits.
  Founder or community voice.

### How to Assign a Sophistication Level

1. **Scan the Meta Ad Library** output for the client's category.
   Count how many competitors run the same claim. More competitors
   on the same claim = higher level.
2. **Scan customer reviews and Reddit threads.** Do customers
   already use the language of the claim? If yes, the market has
   absorbed it. Level 3+.
3. **Check for mechanism fatigue.** If competitors already cite
   mechanisms and customers reference them in reviews ("I switched
   because of the methylated form..."), you are at Level 4+.
4. **Default assumption:** 7 and 8-figure Shopify e-commerce in
   health, wellness, supplements, or consumer products is almost
   always Level 3-5. Level 1-2 categories are rare.

Every persona gets a sophistication level assigned. Every script
references the level. A Level 5 script that leads with a plain
benefit claim gets rejected at QC.

## RESEARCH ANCHORS — DESIRE / SYMPTOM / PROBLEM

Every pain point must be classified under one of three research
anchors. This classification governs how hooks
are built.

**Desire** — what the customer WANTS but does not have yet.
> "I want my dog to run around like a puppy again."
> "I want to wake up without feeling like I got hit by a truck."

Hook angle: paint the after-state. Use transformation and identity
language.

**Symptom** — what the customer NOTICES happening.
> "My dog is struggling to jump on the couch."
> "I'm waking up at 3am three nights a week."

Hook angle: name the specific noticed event. The customer hasn't
diagnosed the cause, so the hook introduces the cause as a reveal.

**Problem** — what the customer KNOWS is wrong.
> "My dog has arthritis."
> "I have sleep apnea."

Hook angle: agitate what they already know and offer a new solution
angle.

**Rotate anchors across every campaign.** Do not build all hooks on
one anchor. A campaign using only Problem-anchor hooks misses the
Desire-anchored audience and vice versa.

## MESSAGE STRATEGIES LIBRARY

Every script, hook, and persona profile cites from a shared library
of 50 persuasion strategies (MS1-MS50), drawn from Schwartz,
Cialdini, Whitman, Miller, Heath & Heath, Hopkins, Halbert, and
Ogilvy.

Read the library at `references/MESSAGE_STRATEGIES.md`.

Every persona gets 3-5 "best MS numbers" attached. Every script
references the MS numbers it is executing. Every QC pass verifies
the script actually lands them.

This is the shared vocabulary. Use it.

## UNIVERSAL FORMATTING RULES

These rules apply to every output: research, scripts, static briefs,
landing page copy, and anything else generated here. No exceptions.

**1. No em dashes in generated output.**

Use ' - ' (space-hyphen-space), ': ', or restructure the sentence.
The only exception is verbatim brand quotes or customer review text
where the em dash exists in the original source material. In that
case, preserve the source punctuation exactly as written. This rule
applies to all generated text: annotations, gap notes, summary
lines, hook copy, script copy, cell contents, and return messages.

**2. Every hook must be ad-ready.**

You should be able to copy-paste any hook directly onto a static ad
and have it work as-is. No short fragmented
phrase-period-phrase patterns unless the fragment is genuinely
punchy and intentional. Default to complete, natural sentences that
read as real ad copy. This standard applies everywhere a hook
appears: the hook list, video openers, static headlines, and landing
page headers.

Bad: `Licensed Mossy Oak Bottomland. Not knockoff. Not generic.`
Good: `This is licensed Mossy Oak Bottomland camo - not a knockoff, not generic`

Bad: `New drop. Limited run. Real camo.`
Good: `The new limited-run drop is real Bottomland camo, not another generic knockoff`

The test: read the hook out loud. If it sounds like a bullet list,
rewrite it as a sentence.

## CORE AD PRINCIPLES

**1. Assume No One Knows or Cares About Your Brand**
Your ad must quickly communicate relevance and benefit. Most people
scroll fast and give brands zero benefit of the doubt.

**2. Keep It Simple**
Avoid jargon, complicated language, or cleverness that confuses.
Clear, direct communication of what the product does and the benefit
is the standard.

**3. Congruency Is King**
All elements (copy, visuals, tone) must align and reinforce the same
message. One ad, one idea, one CTA. Competing elements kill
conversions.

**4. Your Customers Are Your Best Copywriters**
Use actual customer language, especially from long, positive
reviews. Find genuine emotional phrases that resonate with your
audience. Organize reviews by length and extract emotional language
and headline ideas.

**5. Make People Feel Something**
Emotive copy that connects on a personal level drives action. Use
insider phrases, authentic expressions, and language pulled directly
from the audience.

**6. Statics Need a Hook — Use Psychology-Based Design**
Key message must stand out instantly. People scan ads in
milliseconds. Use size, font weight, highlights, color, and visual
hierarchy to control what they see first. Bold, italics, distortion,
arrows: use design to stop the scroll.

**7. Reverse Engineer Winning Copy from Competitors**
Study high-engagement competitor ads. Sort by engagement metrics to
find what's actually working. Do not rely on longest-running ads as
a success indicator, they can be misleading.

**8. Be Clear and Concise**
Clarity over cleverness. Make the transformation or benefit obvious.
Simple language that everyday people understand. Simplify headlines
without losing meaning.

**9. Repurpose Winning Messages Across Formats**
Proven headlines and hooks get reused as static ads, video hooks,
headline bars, and more. This maximizes creative efficiency and
maintains consistent messaging across formats.

## WHAT HIGH-PERFORMING CREATIVES DO DIFFERENTLY

- Test multiple hooks
- Rotate formats
- Refresh visuals quickly
- Iterate fast
- Do not obsess over one ad

Creative fatigue is inevitable. Today's winner will die. Speed
creates more tests, more tests create more winners.

## THE MASTER QC STANDARD

Every creative output must pass ALL of these before it is saved:

**1. The Cold Customer Test (Primary)**
Would a cold customer, no prior brand knowledge, mid-scroll on Meta,
click to the next step? If no, revise.

**2. The 2-Second Test**
Would this hook earn attention in the first 2 seconds without sound?
If no, revise.

**3. The One-Message Test**
Is there exactly one message, one hook, and one CTA? If there are
competing ideas, remove them.

**4. The Research Anchor Test**
Is every hook, claim, and pain point grounded in actual market
research: customer reviews, competitor analysis, or verified
statistics? If it came from imagination, it does not belong.

**5. The Native Feel Test**
Does this look and sound like something a real person would post
organically on Meta? Or does it look like a produced commercial? If
it looks like an ad, revise.

**6. The Persona Match Test**
Is this creative built for a specific person with a specific
problem? Generic output that could apply to anyone applies to no
one.

---

## WHAT FAILURE LOOKS LIKE

These are execution failures, not creative differences. Any output
containing these should not be saved:

- Weak hook that doesn't call out a specific audience
- No implied benefit in the hook
- Product or brand mentioned before tension is built
- Copy based on assumptions, not research
- Multiple competing messages in one ad
- Generic language ("transform," "discover," "unlock," "journey")
- Hook weaker than the research headline it was built from
- CTA that is pushy or unclear
- Ad that would only work if the audience already knew the brand
